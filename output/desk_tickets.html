Reflection

My read on how the Campus Customs team actually performed on the August 31 board. Evidence comes from the Actual sections on the 101, 102 and 103 tabs and from the Cash tab.

1 · Agent performance by ticket

B Ticket 101: right diagnosis, unfinished customer outcome

The team nailed the hard part. Ticket 101 looks like a one-tee order, but the real problem was upstream: CC-TEE-WHITE S was at 0, the only apparel vendor (Bulldog Print Co) wouldn't ship, and the reason was an $840 invoice that was already 3 days overdue on the shop's own date (2026-08-31). Inventory found the block with check_vendor_can_ship(1). Accounting checked cash and existing approvals before filing, then requested exactly $840.00, an amount the MCP tool read from invoices, not one the model typed. No money moved until I signed. In the Cash tab you can see the clean $3,400.00 → $2,560.00 step tied to payment #1.

Where it falls short is the part a retailer actually gets judged on. Tauhid never heard from us: Customer Service was never called, so there's no reply draft. Even after the invoice was paid, nobody went back to draft the reorder, so we spent $840 to unblock a vendor and then never placed the order. The ticket shows resolved because our backend rule says "nothing is waiting on a human," but the customer still doesn't have a tee. That's an ops definition of done, not a customer one. 27,434 tokens is reasonable for the work done.

A Ticket 102: the model run

Rent is the cleanest workflow in the shop, and the agents treated it that way. Boss → Facilities → Accounting matched my plan exactly. It was also the one time the full-mesh design worked as intended: Facilities handed off to Accounting directly instead of bouncing back through the Boss. Facilities confirmed the $2,400 and the 2026-09-02 due date from leases rather than trusting the "rent due in 2 days" email, and Accounting re-read get_lease(1) before filing approval #2. After I approved it, the lease's next_due rolled to 2026-10-02 and checking went $2,560.00 → $160.00.

Bonus points for something going wrong correctly. Facilities' first draft_message call was rejected by the tool's author check, and it retried successfully. That's the guardrail doing its job and the agent recovering on its own. The only extra was a landlord draft I hadn't planned for. It's harmless, never sent, and arguably good landlord relations.

C+ Ticket 103: smart answers, expensive org chart

The content was strong. Accounting priced a 20% bulk deal at $46.40/unit, $928 total, $24.40/unit margin ($488) over the $22 cost. It probed the floor (the tool puts the cost line at about 62% off) and said clearly that no discount policy exists, so a human has to own the number. Inventory confirmed 8 of 20 in size M (12 short). It also found the most interesting insight of the whole run: the hoodie restock is blocked by the same invoice #501 as ticket 101. That's a real cross-ticket dependency, and a human skimming three separate tickets could easily miss it.

The coordination was poor, though. The Boss fanned out to three specialists at once, and then Customer Service acted like a second manager and re-delegated to Accounting and Inventory. The result was 5 hand-offs instead of 2, Accounting and Inventory each running twice, and 51,807 tokens, about 2× either other ticket, for the same facts. After all that, Customer Service declined to draft anything for the Yale AI Club, because the discount wasn't authorized. That's defensible on compliance, but a good retail rep would still send "8 are ready now, pricing for 20 is with my manager." The ticket also landed resolved while the actual business decision (what discount do we offer?) is still open. That's a false green on the board.

Overall: 3/3 resolved, $3,240 out and $160 left, with every dollar matching cash_accounts, and zero money moved without a human. The controls were excellent. The weak spots were delegation discipline and closing the loop with customers, which traces straight back to my Customer Service and Boss prompts being one sentence each.

3 · What would have been simpler as one agent

Honestly, most of this board. Campus Customs is 9 tables and a few dozen rows, and all 22 MCP tools fit easily in one agent's context. Look at the token bill: 105,671 tokens across three tickets (27,434 + 26,430 + 51,807). Every hand-off re-sends a prompt, a task and fresh get_ticket_details calls. That's why get_ticket_details ran 13 times across the 3 tickets, when 3 would have done.

  • 102 is a 3-call job for one agent: get_lease(1) → request_payment_approval("rent", 1) → update_ticket_status. We used 12 model calls and 3 agent loops.
  • 103 is basically one call: quote_bulk_price("CC-HOOD-NAVY", 20, pct, "M") already returns price, margin, stock on hand and shortfall, followed by one draft_message. One agent couldn't have "re-delegated to itself," so the 51,807-token sprawl wouldn't have happened.
  • 101 benefits most from one brain: the same context that sees stock = 0 would also see the invoice block, file the request and draft Tauhid's reply. The reorder and the reply fell through the cracks between agents.

What the multi-agent design does buy is separation of duties. Only Accounting can request a payment, just like a store manager in a retail chain can't cut a check. But in this app that control is actually enforced in code: tool filtering per role, plus approve_and_pay being hidden from every agent and refusing agent approvers. So I'd keep that permission model and run one agent with role-scoped tool sets per ticket type. I'd only bring back multiple agents when volume or parallelism justifies the coordination tax, for example a back-to-school rush with 200 tickets.

5 · Three new problems this team can't solve, and what I'd build
  1. The $160 cash crunch: October rent and no revenue. The Cash tab ends at $160.00 with $2,400 rent due 2026-10-02. The team can see the gap, but there's nothing it can do about it. There's no sales data, no receivables and no forecast, because "cash only goes out." Even the Yale AI Club deal (about $928 at 20% off) can't be booked as income.
    Build: a sales/orders table plus POS ingest; tools record_sale, get_sales_by_day, forecast_cash(days) and list_receivables; and a Finance/FP&A agent that projects runway, ranks which bills to pay first by due date and vendor criticality, and flags "you need $2,240 of sales by 10/2." Any deposit would go through the same human approval queue.
  2. Actually fulfilling orders and receiving stock. The agents can see inventory but can't move it. There's no way to reserve Tauhid's tee, ship the 8 hoodies we do have for 103, or check in Bulldog Print Co's shipment when it lands. That's why 101 shows "resolved" with no tee in anyone's hands. Reorders are drafts in a drafts table, not real purchase orders with a cost and a status.
    Build: a purchase_orders table plus tools create_purchase_order (with total cost, routed to the human approval queue as a "purchase"), receive_shipment (increments inventory.qty and creates the vendor invoice), reserve_stock/fulfill_order (decrements qty, supports partial shipments like 8 of 20), and a Fulfillment/Receiving agent. "Resolved" would then mean the customer has the product.
  3. Owning the discount decision and talking to the customer. Ticket 103 exposed that there's no pricing policy anywhere in the database.
